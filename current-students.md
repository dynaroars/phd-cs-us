- [Advice to a Beginning Graduate Student](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~mblum/research/pdf/grad.html) — Manuel Blum.
- [Fifteen pieces of advice I wish my PhD advisor had given me](https://www-net.cs.umass.edu/kurose/talks/student_keynote_final.pdf) — Jim Kurose.
- [Ten tips for PhD students](https://utstat.toronto.edu/reid/sta2212s/2023/WittenGradTips.pdf) — Daniela Witten.
- [The Tao of PhD](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse590x/22wi/) — University of Washington seminar on navigating graduate school.
- [Graduate Study in the Computer and Mathematical Sciences](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~oleary/gradstudy/gradstudy.html) — Dianne P. O’Leary’s guide to graduate study and getting started in research.
- [The Redbook](https://github.com/austenmc/theredbook) — a historical graduate student guide to Stanford CS.
- [Time Management](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~dahlin/bookshelf/timetalk.htm) — Randy Pausch’s talk.
- [Expectation setting](https://www.eugenevinitsky.com/posts/expectation-setting/) — Eugene Vinitsky on discussing goals and working arrangements with an advisor.
- [Graduate student mentoring guide and shared-expectations templates](https://rackham.umich.edu/faculty-and-staff/resources-for-directors/mentoring/) — Michigan Rackham Graduate School.
- [Support when changing advisors](https://rackham.umich.edu/about/directory/graduate-student-and-program-consultation-services/transitional-support-for-change-of-advisors/) — Michigan Rackham’s example of a departmental transition process.
- [Advocacy services at Cornell](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/policies/advocacy-services/) and [DoingWell at MIT](https://doingwell.mit.edu/support-3/) — examples of campus advocacy, counseling, and well-being services.
- [PhD Life](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/dc/phdlife/blog/) — graduate student blog from the [University of Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/).
- [ROARS Lab Advising Guide](advising.pdf) — ThanhVu Nguyen’s account of advising expectations and lab culture.
- [Graduate funding and enrollment](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/financial-support/graduate-funding-and-enrollment/), [leaves of absence](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/policies/leaves-of-absence/), and [disability policies](https://gradschool.cornell.edu/policies/disability-policies/) — Cornell examples of practical questions to take to your own graduate school.
- [Graduate fellowships and application examples](prospective-students.md#funding-and-fellowships) — funding resources for applicants and eligible enrolled students.
- [Individual development plan](https://grad.umn.edu/academic-career-support/individual-development-plan) — University of Minnesota templates for setting research and career goals with a mentor.

## Research and reading

- [How to Do Great Research](https://researchhowto.wordpress.com/) — Nick Feamster and Alex Gray on developing ideas and communicating results.
- [How to Do Research](https://visionbook.mit.edu/how_to_do_research.html) — research advice from the authors of *Foundations of Computer Vision*.
- [An Opinionated Guide to ML Research](http://joschu.net/blog/opinionated-guide-ml-research.html) — John Schulman.
- [The Art of Doing Good Research](https://blender.cs.illinois.edu/research/ResearchSkills.pptx) — Heng Ji’s slides on research skills and collaboration.
- [How to Have a Bad Career in Research/Academia](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pattrsn/talks/BadCareer.pdf) — David Patterson.
- [Useful Thoughts about Research](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/htk/phdadvice/) — H. T. Kung.
- [You and Your Research](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html) — Richard Hamming.
- [Collected Advice on Research and Writing](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/user/mleone/web/how-to.html) — Mark Leone’s collection of guides on graduate study and research.
- [AI Research Experiences](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1uvAbEhbgS_M-uDMTzmOWRlYxqCkogKRXdbKYYT98ooc/edit) — Pranav Rajpurkar’s course book.
- [How to Read a Paper](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs245/readings/how-to-read-a-paper.pdf) — Srinivasan Keshav’s three-pass method.
- [How to Read a Technical Paper](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~jason/advice/how-to-read-a-paper.html) — Jason Eisner.
- [Open, rigorous and reproducible research](https://stanforddatascience.github.io/best-practices/) — Stanford Data Science’s practitioner handbook.
- [Good Enough Practices in Scientific Computing](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1005510) — Greg Wilson and collaborators on organizing data, code, and projects.
- [Introduction to the Responsible Conduct of Research](https://ori.hhs.gov/education/products/RCRintro/) — Office of Research Integrity materials on data management, collaboration, and authorship.
- [The Missing Semester of Your CS Education](https://missing.csail.mit.edu/) — practical skills for working with shells, version control, and reproducible environments.
- [Zotero documentation](https://www.zotero.org/support/) — organize papers, annotations, citations, and shared bibliographies.
- [ORCID for researchers](https://info.orcid.org/researchers/) — maintain a persistent identifier for your academic work.

## Writing, publishing, and peer review

- [Write the Paper First](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/~jason/advice/write-the-paper-first.html) — Jason Eisner on using a draft to clarify a research project.
- [How to write a good CVPR submission](https://billf.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/cvprPapers.pdf) — Bill Freeman.
- [Ten Simple Rules for Mathematical Writing](https://www.mit.edu/~dimitrib/Ten_Rules.html) — Dimitri Bertsekas.
- [Notes on writing](https://people.csail.mit.edu/fredo/PUBLI/writing.pdf) — Frédo Durand.
- [How to write a (hopefully good) paper](https://mri.beckman.illinois.edu/resources/good_paper.pdf) — Martin Vetterli.

- [How to Write a Lot](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/4441031) — Paul Silvia on making time for scholarly writing.
- [ACM publications policies](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies) and [artifact review and badging](https://www.acm.org/publications/policies/artifact-review-and-badging-current).
- [ICML reviewer tutorial](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2022/ReviewerTutorial) and [CVPR reviewer guidelines](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines) — conference examples.
- [How to be a good reviewer](https://web.archive.org/web/20260209110718/https://cvpr2022.thecvf.com/sites/default/files/2021-11/How%20to%20be%20a%20good%20reviewer-tutorials%20for%20cvpr2022%20reviewers.pptx.pdf) — archived CVPR 2022 reviewer tutorial slides.
- [How to write a good review](https://sites.google.com/view/making-reviews-great-again/) — CVPR 2020 tutorial.
- [How to write a reviewer report](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ChanGroup/write_review.html) — Stanley Chan.
- [Conference Submission Review Policy](https://www.sigplan.org/Resources/Policies/Review/) — ACM SIGPLAN’s guidance on fair review and conflicts of interest.
